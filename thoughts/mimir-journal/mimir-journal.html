Overview

Can a system learn, or only retrieve?

Mimir is a research project on long-term memory for AI — built over three months (February 27 to May 30, 2026) around a single uncomfortable question: can a system actually learn continuously from a stream of conversation, or does it only ever retrieve?

Retrieval, it turns out, is largely solved. A well-tuned hybrid of BM25, cosine similarity, and a cross-encoder reranker already places the right evidence in the top-10 over 97% of the time. The open frontier is everything that retrieval is not: writing experience into a model's weights, predicting latent structure, binding events into episodes, reasoning over time. Mimir is the record of chasing that frontier — what worked, what failed loudly, and why.

The method is deliberately dual. On the formal side, Lean 4 proofs establish the dynamics a memory system should obey — Banach fixed-point stability, Lyapunov energy descent, a derived stability boundary — across two sorry-free libraries totaling 335 theorems. On the empirical side, every claim is forced through the LongMemEval / MemoryAgentBench benchmarks under the unforgiving SubEM metric. The two halves were meant to reinforce each other. Sometimes they did; sometimes the proofs stayed pristine while reality refused to cooperate — a tension that became one of the project's central lessons.

The journey runs across eleven families. It opens with agentic ReAct retrieval (an early winner that later measured negative against a single deterministic function) and settles on the Engram/foundry recall() core, the canonical system that reached a #1-on-leaderboard 60.33%. Around it orbit the supporting and the abandoned: knowledge-graph and information-extraction work that fought extraction-vocabulary mismatch; temporal reasoning that kept colliding with the difference between when an event was discussed and when it happened; episodic event-binding that was correct but score-neutral; dual-extraction observations still alive at the close. The hardest mandate — continual learning, parametric memory, and JEPA world-models — was brutal almost everywhere, with test-time training the one flicker of life. The Lean/energy half stayed mathematically perfect but empirically vacuous at scale. And a final post-processing, routing, and evaluation-integrity thread kept the whole enterprise honest — removing hard-coded shortcuts even when it cost points. This is that record.

The eleven families
Agentic / ReAct Retrieval. Tool-calling agents that iteratively retrieve evidence via multi-step reasoning loops — the MABench/FC monolithic winner (cooc+triadic+decompose+CR+bm25_hops, SubEM 39), the composed-agent refactor, the 7-tool AgenticRetriever, the Chronos tool-calling agents (h=3/h=5), and the planned Traces→Deterministic→Distilled-SLM pipeline. — Started as the project's first serious system in the MABench era (March, SubEM 39 best on FactConsolidation). A POC on LME looked promising (38.3% BM25-only). But once a single deterministic recall() function was built, the agent measured -5 SubEM against it (and vs direct BM25); every Chronos agent variant tied or regressed against plain chunks. ABANDONED for LME — 'agent HURTS', declared dead. The trace-distillation follow-on was rendered moot before phases 2-4 were built.
Engram / Foundry Retrieval (recall() core). The hybrid-retrieval workhorse: a single recall() function doing BM25 + cosine RRF + cross-encoder rerank, with turn-level (not chunk-level) BM25 indexing, predicate dispatch, reranker ablation (mxbai winner, Qwen3-Reranker dead), chunk-size and aggregation-overflow sweeps, and the long exp1xx micro-experiment series. — Emerged as the foundation around 2026-04-20 after the structured-store era stalled, immediately delivering 43.33% ctx0. Turn-level BM25 closed a 35-point gap with MABench and became standard. Component ablation proved ranking was SOLVED (mxbai puts 97.2% reachable in top-10) and recall was the bottleneck (53% unreachable). Micro-experiments (exp147-155, evidence-order/truncation/top_k) all plateaued or regressed — the configuration space was exhausted. WON: this is the canonical system that hit 60.33% honest 5-ctx (#1 on LME(S*) leaderboard) and 56% on LME-500. Folded every other family's gains in or out around it.
Structured / Knowledge-Graph Retrieval. Entity- and triple-based retrieval as an alternative or supplement to text search: StructuredFactStore as a 3rd RRF channel, entity-prefilter, KnowledgeGraph (rustworkx PPR), HyperedgeIntersection, hard entity-first filtering, the bottom-up 14-op KG Query Engine, MSL query DSL, and the Palantir-Foundry ontology influence. — A dominant March-April thrust. Entity-prefilter was a genuine win (+6%, the 22.67% jump). But pure structured query replaced BM25 badly (-7%), the structured store was net-neutral across Exp A/B/C (~22%), hard entity-first filtering caused the SubEM=9 catastrophe, and the MSL graph answered 30/60 questions ALL WRONG due to ingestion-vs-query vocabulary mismatch. FOLDED-IN as a supplement only (hybrid BM25 + structured fallback reached 31.33%); the standalone-graph dream was ABANDONED — the entity is sound as a unit but extraction-vocabulary consistency was the unbreakable blocker.
Information Extraction & Ontology Design. Turning conversation into structured facts: spaCy/REBEL/GLiNER2/textacy extractors, QA-driven 5W1H extraction, the 28-relation-type schema, the 9-facet shape-based ontology, layered EntityGraph (STATED/INFERRED/DERIVED), the v9 Qwen2.5-7B pipeline, and the dual-extraction (schema→deriver) architecture. — A relentless reduce-hallucination, increase-density campaign. Early IE failed hard: REBEL cost -10 points and swapped relation/object; spaCy gave only 45 unique (S,P) keys; free-form extraction got 1.7% SubEM. The 9-facet ontology + grounding substring check fixed the design (graph = user's words). v9 reached 97.2% entity grounding after the source_text fix (16%→92%). The breakthrough was dual-extraction: schema constrains the deriver so even a 3B model hallucinates zero. WON as architecture (production path: lightweight prompt, 1.7s/chunk), though the layered/typed graph stayed net-neutral on SubEM because of vocabulary mismatch.
Temporal Information Extraction & Reasoning. Date and event handling: Allen interval algebra engine, stateful MSL schema with sub_role, the always-on temporal pipeline (Chat-Time ingestion, proximity re-ranking, code-computed deltas), ADELIE event-extraction prompts, regex date ceilings, _infer_temporal_diff, and the Chronos dual-calendar Event Calendar. — Recognized early as the hardest LME category (35% on LME-500). Many naive attempts failed: temporal pre-filter added zero value, the 3-feature stack regressed, temporal short-circuit hit only 40%, and date-arithmetic modules 'fired on 26/26, all wrong' because chunk timestamps mark when an event was DISCUSSED not when it HAPPENED — the central, recurring failure mode. WON in narrow form: stateful MSL hit 100% multilingual, and code-computed _infer_temporal_diff drove ctx0 to 80%. PARTIALLY FOLDED-IN; the deeper temporal-reasoning gap remains the #1 open bottleneck (79% of temporal failures are retrieval misses).
Episodic Memory & Event Binding. Adding an event/episode index layer over content-addressable retrieval: EventFrames (topic-shift segmentation + temporal contiguity), and the prior-art survey of EM-LLM, REMem, SEEM, and Triadic Memory framed around content-addressable-vs-index-based memory. — A late-April direction sparked by literature survey. The survey crystallized 4 ranked missing capabilities (temporal contiguity, event gists, provenance pointers, surprise segmentation) and the framing that 'our system is 100% content-addressable, needs an index layer'. EventFrames were fully built (47 tests, 5 contract assertions pass) but eval was exactly NEUTRAL — 196/300, matching baseline to the question. FOLDED-IN as correct infrastructure that didn't move SubEM; episodic binding is in place but score-neutral, awaiting the provenance/gist modalities to pay off.
Dual Extraction & Observations. Generating natural-language observations from verified schema and using them as a semantic-search modality, culminating in the 'Search Observations, Serve Turns' pointer architecture where observations bridge retrieval gaps but raw turns are served as evidence. — Grew out of the IE family once schema→deriver proved zero-hallucination. First direct use FAILED at ctx0: observations in the index displaced real turns (82% of vector index became observations) and regressed counting. The insight was to decouple — search observations, resolve to source turn IDs, serve turns. IN-PROGRESS at project end (LME-500 eval running against 280/500), motivated by the finding that 79% of LME-500 failures are retrieval misses that denser observations might bridge. The most-alive non-retrieval research thread at close.
Continual Learning & Parametric Memory. Writing memory into model weights rather than an index: 24 LoRA/full-FT experiments (E01-E24), MEGa gated per-event adapters, Test-Time Training, Titans/neural-memory, the Hebbian bridge, MIRAS/MIRROR design frameworks, parametric-capacity theory, and the Sleeping-LLM (MEMIT wake + LoRA sleep) reference. — The core JEPA/engram research mandate (learning, not retrieval). Brutal across the board: best LoRA was E05 at 10/60 (17%, high variance), bigger models DIVERGED (E23 7B: loss up every epoch, 0/60), Adam destroyed the model, EWC-at-scale hit 9%, all Titans variants regressed. Capacity theory explained why (0.5B ≈ 0.29GB, 33K facts overwhelm it). The one bright spot: TTT broke the 12% offline ceiling at 30% via per-query weight adaptation. Largely ABANDONED for LME (parametric learning a dead end at this scale), but TTT-E2E flagged as the highest-leverage open opportunity.
World Models (JEPA) & Self-Supervised Encoders. JEPA/C-JEPA/VICReg latent-prediction approaches to memory: ConversationJEPA sliding-window latents, JEPA soft-prefix and KV injection, JEPA fine-tune variants, contrastive/dual-space heads, and sbert retrieval baselines (Architecture I) used as the control. — Pursued under the continuous-learning mandate as world-models for multi-hop structure. Consistently FAILED: soft-prefix 0%, KV injection 4% (broke generation), every C-JEPA/VICReg variant 9-23%, and crucially JEPA training DESTROYED sbert's retrieval (loss converged but learned the wrong thing — prediction objective ≠ retrieval metric). The control quietly won: plain sbert over individual facts hit 65%, the best of all 26 JEPA-phase experiments. ABANDONED — JEPA's predictive objective is the wrong abstraction for fact retrieval.
Formal Verification (Lean) & Energy Dynamics. The formal half of MIMIR: Lean 4 proofs of memory dynamics — hermes-memory (75 theorems, Banach fixed-point stability), hermes-NLCDM (27 modules, 260 theorems, 15-phase coupled dynamics, all sorry-free), and the empirical NLCDM/Hopfield energy validation on real LME embeddings. — Built first (Feb-March) as the project's formal foundation and remained mathematically pristine: 0 sorries throughout, energy descent / Lyapunov stability holds 100% in proofs, with a derived stability boundary. But empirical validation (April) was a DEAD END: at LME scale (N=1200) the transfer bound was 1000x too loose, energy couldn't distinguish success from failure (AUC 0.34-0.53; -0.789 vs -0.790), and Hopfield retrieval was strictly worse than cosine everywhere. Even fact-centroids (N=53) didn't rescue it. PROOFS CORRECT BUT VACUOUS — Hopfield energy is content-addressable recall; LME needs reasoning over facts.
Post-Processing, Routing & Evaluation Methodology. Everything between retrieval and score: the recursive memory oracle (D-01 to D-20, SLMs as stateless processors), the code-generator oracle, NEI detection, evidence-gate widening, the memory router (regex and LLM-classifier), dual-tier LLM-as-judge scoring, and eval-integrity work (hard-coding removal, ground-truth bug discovery). — A pragmatic late thread once retrieval plateaued. The recursive oracle was NEUTRAL on ctx0 (D-05 to D-12 all exactly 80%, matching baseline) — enrichment a dead end — but post-processing NEI detection (D-17/D-18) did become 5-ctx best (66.33%). The code oracle worked in isolation (5/5) but was KILLED on LME-500 (0 improved, 2 regressed). The router was validated (98% effective accuracy) projecting 31%→53%. Critically, this family enforced honesty: hard-coded regex computation was REMOVED (196→181/300), and the resulting 60.33% honest baseline still topped the LME(S*) leaderboard. WON as the discipline that kept results trustworthy; the oracle/router pieces mostly folded-in selectively or stayed projected.

The Energy Well: Building a Memory from First Principles

The first week of MIMIR was an act of faith in mathematics. The question I set out to chase was almost arrogant in its ambition: could a long-term memory for an AI be derived — proven correct from physical first principles — rather than merely engineered and tuned until the benchmark numbers looked good? Most memory systems are scaffolding held up by hyperparameters. I wanted bedrock.

So I began with the scaffolding anyway. The first three days (Feb 27–28) were a TDD sprint to build hermes-memory v1: five modules — encoding, recall, contradiction, consolidation, optimizer — each one hardened by adversarial pre-passes before a line of implementation was written. Recall got score-gap adaptive-k depth selection instead of a lazy fixed top-k. Contradiction came out at 1,457 lines after three adversarial passes surfaced 39 findings. Consolidation's build was where the discipline paid for itself: a cascade analysis traced a 24x over-retention bug back to a single hours-vs-days unit confusion (C2). The system had been functionally broken at the dynamics level and the tests caught it. By the time the orchestrator (770 lines) wired all five modules together, the suite stood at 1,455 passing, 0 failures, 8,857 lines of implementation against 19,398 lines of tests. The encoding hardening pass taught me something I'd carry forward: emotion is never a category, it's a dimensional modifier (+0.05 importance) on something else; third-person fact patterns were structurally 14–38% thinner than first-person, a classifier gap no tuning could close.

Then, on March 1, the real pivot. The user handed me a cross-domain synthesis — Hopfield networks, modern dense-associative memory (Ramsauer et al.), contraction theory, Lyapunov composites — and the project became a Lean 4 formalization: hermes-NLCDM. Over a 17-hour final session, Phase 1 closed: Energy.lean, EnergyBounds.lean (eight theorems including a hand-rolled Cauchy-Schwarz via discriminant), and LocalMinima.lean all compiled with zero sorrys. The hard lesson was epistemic humility encoded in syntax — an L2-distance separation hypothesis simply wouldn't close; I had to switch to a similarity-gap formulation to match the literature before the softmax-concentration proof would go through. The proofs don't lie to you the way intuition does.

The Conflict-Resolution Crucible: Chasing the Wrong-Entity Ghost

This was the era of a single, stubborn benchmark and a single, stubborn failure mode. The whole stretch lived inside MABench's FactConsolidation multi-hop set — 100 questions, a ~262K-token fact store, gpt-4o-mini doing the answering — and the question that defined it was deceptively simple: when the same fact exists in an old and a new version, why does the system keep picking the wrong one?

We opened at SubEM 20-22 (cooc + triadic + decompose + temporal) and immediately ran a root-cause diagnostic on the 76-79 wrong answers. The verdict was brutal and clarifying: 94-96% of failures were conflict resolution — WRONG_ENTITY 76/79 (96%). The LLM was retrieving both the stale and the current fact and choosing badly. Retrieval, we assumed, was fine; reasoning was the bottleneck. That assumption would not survive the era.

The first thing that worked was almost embarrassingly simple. Temporal outgestion — sorting retrieved facts by recency and tagging them [older]/[newer] with a "prefer the newer fact" instruction — lifted us to SubEM 22, a new high. But every attempt to be cleverer about conflict backfired. Hard [SUPERSEDED] cosine tags at 0.85/0.93: SubEM 19. Soft [CONFLICTS WITH] hints: 19. The lesson, repeated until it bruised: the problem was never signal strength, it was false positives. Cosine similarity at 0.96 between same-template, different-entity facts simply cannot tell a conflict from a sibling.

Write-time supersession became the next obsession. spaCy dep-parse triples gave 45 edges from 18K facts — 1% coverage, "essentially empty." OpenIE swung the other way: 13,016 edges that over-removed so aggressively the system deleted "John Witherspoon affiliated with Islam" and regressed to SubEM 20. Between the famine and the flood, two honest wins emerged. A copular predicate fix ((Abbiati, be, basketball) → (Abbiati, sport, basketball)) recovered +3 by stopping different attributes registering as conflicts. And moving CR to query time, per-hop with acl-aware extraction jumped us to SubEM 25, then 30 once a triple-extractor blind-spot (prep-chains like "works in the field of") and a Tier-1 predicate-cardinality gate (exclusive vs. additive lemma lists) landed. SubEM 30 was the project high to date.

Then came the temptations and the corrections. A template-regex hack covering 95.4% of the benchmark's fixed sentence patterns spiked to SubEM 28 — and was reverted on sight as pure overfitting. A Bayesian belief system briefly showed 23, then dissolved into a --force flag that was dead code, meaning months of evals had silently loaded stale caches without the belief .pkl files. A separate catastrophe — SubEM 4 — turned out to be the openai/gpt-4o-mini OpenRouter prefix, not the code at all. The era's quiet hero may be the persistent ingestion cache (eval_cache.py) that cut iteration from 2 hours to 2 seconds and let us trust our numbers again.

The single most important reframe was the ceiling analysis. We had believed 26 was near-optimal. A BFS over 18,332 raw facts said the real ceiling was at least 64 — and a later hop-by-hop replay pushed it to ~98 (only 1-2 truly unsolvable). That overturned everything: this was not a reasoning problem, it was a retrieval problem. 70% of failures happened at Hop 1, where embedding retrieval returned same-template, wrong-entity facts. The deep taxonomy made it stark: 88% of 72 failures were entity discrimination. Cosine encodes template structure, not entity identity.

Dream consolidation died here, conclusively and across five configurations: recency-weighted (SubEM 6), GLiNER entity-safe gating (7), explore-only bridge generation (22) — all wrecked by nrem_repulsion corrupting the 17K-memory store. Closing the journal

What won, and what comes next

Retrieval, done carefully, beat everything we built on top of it.

MIMIR — Closing the Journal (Feb 27 – May 30, 2026)

Three months, ~200 experiments, two disciplines (Lean proofs + Python eval on LongMemEval/SubEM). The honest summary: retrieval, done carefully, beat everything we built on top of it.

What won

The honest, no-hard-coding retrieval baseline — BM25 + cosine RRF + cross-encoder rerank, plus EventFrames and turn-level indexing — reached 60.33% SubEM (181/300) on LME-S* 5-ctx, topping every named system: GPT-4.1-mini (55.7%), MIRIX (51.0%), HippoRAG-v2 (50.7%), Gemini-2.0-Flash (47.0%). On the full benchmark, 56% (280/500). That is the deliverable. It started life at 43.33% (recall()) and 16.67% (plain cosine, mabench era); the whole arc was disciplined retrieval engineering, not novel memory mechanisms.

Correct but vacuous

The Lean formalizations (75 + 260 theorems, 0 sorry) are correct: energy descent / Lyapunov stability hold 100% in proof. But at LME scale (N≈1200 dense turns) they are empirically vacuous — transfer bound 1000× loose, alignment gap δ negative, energy-as-predictor AUC 0.34–0.53 (success vs failure energy: −0.789 vs −0.790). Even shrinking to N=53 fact centroids only made the theory prettier, never predictive. Hopfield energy is content-addressable completion; LME demands reasoning over facts. Right math, wrong abstraction.

The dead-ends that taught the most

  • Parametric / LoRA continual learning — every scale failed (1.5B ceiling 17% and high-variance; 7B diverged to 0/60). Knowledge injection via weights is a knife-edge; absorption, not refusal, is the wall.
  • Hopfield/NLCDM energy — see above; strictly worse than cosine at retrieval.
  • ReAct / agentic retrieval — −5 SubEM vs a single deterministic pass.
  • Code oracle — net −2 on LME-500; it corrupted already-correct answers.
  • Hard-coded regex — the +15 it bought (196 vs 181) was the benchmark computing the answer, not memory. Deleting it was the most important commit of the project.

Where it points

Retrieval is solved enough. The open frontier is continuous learning — systems that learn from a conversation stream (inner/outer loop, SLM-as-oracle, world models), not ones that re-rank a frozen corpus. TTT (30% probe, 2.5× the offline ceiling) is the only parametric signal that survived; it's the thread worth pulling. The goal was never a leaderboard — it was universal, evergreen memory. The benchmark was the flashlight, never the room.
